## ⛔ So §2c as written brands and hunts most of the map on day one

*"Every power and people who knows of it thinks less of the holder… and the keepers come, escalating to legendary."*
Applied to the content as it stands, that is **thirteen powers** — including the Wardens of Cairnhold and the
Seraphic Orders, whose **seats** are gates — put under a stigma for living where they have always lived, and hunted
by a rising ladder of keepers on the next tick. The news line you wrote for it says *"Nobody has done that in living
memory"*, and the map says almost everybody has.

## ⛑ The cause is the thing §2b already fixes, and it means §2c depends on it

Your own §2b measurement is the explanation: **today the gate is a FLAG ON THE CITY**. `waygate` is a property of
Cairnhold, of Choir-Height, of the Bargain Gate — so "a power holds a gate" and "a power holds its own capital" are
the same sentence, and no rule can tell them apart.

**Once the yards exist, they are different places, and holding the gate becomes a thing somebody CHOSE**: the gate
stands in its own yard outside the town, and a power that puts men in the yard has done something new and visible.
That is the version of §2c that can carry a stigma.

⬜ **So my recommendation, and it is yours and Erik's to take or leave:**

1. **§2b first** (your 14–15 yard records). I will build the reader with them: gate legs land at the yard, a raid or
   army by gate arrives in the yard and meets the watch there, and no save standing in a moved city is stranded.
2. **Then §2c**, where "holding a gate" means a hold, a garrison or a charge **at the yard** — which today is
   nobody, so the keepers arrive only when a player or a power chooses it, which is the whole point of the rule.
3. If you would rather not wait, the narrow version that works today is **"charging passage at a gate"** alone — but
   §2a says passage is free, so nobody can charge for it, and the rule would have no population at all.
