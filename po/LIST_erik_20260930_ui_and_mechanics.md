| 7 | **The hold's content** | *"The content itself is a bit scattered still."* Build mixes the room line, a craft picker, the refusal paragraph, features by category and the build form; Store & money stacks the runs table, the stored line, a stock rule and a second "moving the store" table. | One order on every tab: **what it is now → what you can do → the detail**. | 8 | **Building out the legs** | *"It says the legs are being built but no one is at it? Cy, a maintenance bot, was delegated to work that."* | **A bug.** The growth job counts hands posted to it, and Cy's delegation doesn't reach it (the same shape as `holdBack`: a write the reader doesn't see). | 9 | **A keep on a moving hold** | *"It said I can't have a keep on a moving hold."* | `hullable: false` is being applied to **every** moving frame, but it was written for boats. A walking hold on legs can carry a keep (a walking fortress is exactly the fantasy). **Rule:** a feature says which frames can carry it. Keep, gate, muster yard, ward line: any frame but a hull. Mine, quarry, grave ground, reclamation bowl: rooted only (they're dug into the ground). Waygate: never built. |
| 10 | **Raised hands as individuals** | *"The raised hands need to be individual units I can apply to various tasks (split or combined)."* Today "hands" is a count on the hold. | Each hand becomes a **person-row** (unnamed until you name them: "a hand from the Crossing", level, what they're good at). They appear in *Who does what here* and can be dragged or assigned one at a time: two to the legs, one to the watch, one to foraging; or all to one job. A job's output adds up its people. Same for a raised band's soldiers. (Bands already have people; this makes a hold's hands work the same way.) |

| 11 | **Bottom of the Holdings page** | *"The bottom of the page has content that could probably better go somewhere else."* The Capacity block (At your side, Running things for you, A following) and the Personnel block sit under the holds. | They're about **you and your people**, not your holds: move them to the **Party** screen (At your side, A following) and to whichever screen holds the charges (#14). | 12 | **"4 of keep owed" / "in arrears 4"** | *"Not sure because I can't seem to do anything about it."* | **A bug and a missing door.** Measured on origin: arrears are only ever *added* (an unpaid keep, or an unpaid market fee when the keeper sells: `holdings.js` ~1757 and ~1803), and **nothing in the engine or app ever pays them down.** So they only grow. Fix: (a) each pass, the hold pays its arrears first out of what it earns before anything else; (b) a **"Pay it now — 4 crystal"** button on the review row and the hold card, from your purse; (c) the row says *why* it's owed ("the keeper couldn't pay the stall at Millbrook on day 112"). |
| 13 | **Cy has the same charge twice** | *"Cy somehow picked up 2 of the same delegation as a job."* Two charges, both "survey and plan expansion of the Standing Annex…". | **A bug:** a charge for the same person, the same place and the same purpose is written twice (the GM's op and the button, or two beats, with nothing checking). Two systems for the same work, so Cy works one and the legs count the other. A charge whose purpose is a hold's growth should **be** that hold's growth job, with Cy on it. |
| 14 | **Where the Jobs tab belongs** | *"The jobs tab might make more sense being part of your band or party screen? or maybe holding... not sure where."* Then: *"there are also jobs from people in places we visit, so they aren't just tied to holds."* | **Keep one home for work, and show each job where it also belongs.** The Jobs tab stays as the place for the **Board** (work offered by people in the places you visit) and **Post a job**, renamed **Work** so it reads as "what's being asked and what's being done". Every **charge** (someone doing something for you) also shows **on the person's card** (Party / People: "what they're doing for you") and, when it names a hold, **on that hold** in *Who does what here*. One record, three places it can be seen, one place it's managed. |
