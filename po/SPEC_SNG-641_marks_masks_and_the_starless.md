## §1 — ⛔ FIRST, THE CORRECTION: SNG-640 WAS WRITTEN WITHOUT R40 AND R41

Erik pointed me at his arrival ruling. There are two, and SNG-640 contradicted one of them.

- **R40a** (`po/RULING_sovereign_arcs.md`): a fed Sovereign is a **beneficiary**. Arcs keep their own causes.
- **R40b.2:** *"A Sovereign's supply state is DERIVED from its arc's stage, not the other way round."*
- **R40b.4:** agents push as ordinary figures. *"Nothing marks them as an agent in the data, and that is the point."*
- **R41** (`po/RULING_sovereign_arrivals.md`): at stage 3 it arrives diminished and **acts**. It pushes the arc back and
  goes after the people opposing it. At stage 4 it arrives in its final form. There are three endings: slain, you fail,
  or it recrosses. The anti-Sovereigns know about it and hunt it. Counterweights starve it without knowing.

⚠️ **SNG-640 §5 had C13 lowering a fed-state counter directly each time a supply-line power broke.** That contradicts
R40b.2, so it is replaced:

> **C13, corrected:** breaking, taking or turning a supply-line power is a **deed against the arc**. It enters
> `arcContests` like any figure's deed, and it pushes the stage of the arc that feeds that Sovereign back. The supply
> state then follows the stage, as R40b.2 requires. The arrival is R41's: `sovereignFormFor` at stage 3 and stage 4.
> ⛔ No new counter.

⚑ **`feedsGM` on a power does not break R40b.4.** A power is not an agent. SNG-640 already recorded that a thieves' guild
carries a Sovereign's freight without one member being an agent. The field is GM-only and is never shown to the player.
No figure gains an agent flag.

⚠️ **The seat count. SNG-640 said "six open seats", and that was wrong.** I quoted the lore's line from before the
Hollow King and the Unbodied were written. The lore lists seven axes and three Sovereigns are authored, so **four seats
are open by the lore's own table.** Erik expects three, and I cannot find where the difference comes from (§7.1).

## §2 — ⛑ THE PRINCIPLE: CONFIRM THE LINE, NEVER THE HAND

The lore sets the bar: *"You detect a run of decisions that are all individually reasonable and cumulatively
impossible."* So every mark below passes one test. **Found alone, each mark has an ordinary explanation.** The
player's own collecting is what makes the marks mean something.

| found | what the player can know | what the GM may say |
|---|---|---|
| **1 mark** | an oddity | only what is in front of them |
| **2 marks, same power** | a pattern | *"You've seen this before."* |
| **3 marks, same power** | ✅ **the power is a line.** Something beyond it is being fed | the agent confirmed (the lore's *"Confirm an AGENT"*): *"The fence works for someone who pays for sleepers."* |
| **the same mark in 2 regions, through 2 different powers** | ⚑ **the same hand**, 200 miles apart | *"The same mark. Nobody here has heard of the other place."* |
| a Sovereign's name | ⛔ **never** from the GM | only from a person who knows: an anti-Sovereign (R41c), a Precursor, or the thing itself when it arrives |

⚑ **The marks are consistent across saves and never overlap between Sovereigns.** A player who learns the hollow coin in
one game will recognise it in the next. That consistency is the reward for paying attention. The world's knowledge can
carry between saves, so it has to be the same knowledge each time.

⚑ **Each Sovereign's marks come from its hunger.** Once a player understands what the marks have in common, they can
predict where the next one will be. That is how a player works it out in stages without ever being told.

## §4 — ⚑ LUCIFER'S MASK

> **Erik:** *"We should probably refer to Lucifer as another name… he would be able to masquerade as a Seraph, but he
> could be discovered if he doesn't bleed golden nanites."*

**Proposed name: Eosphor, the Dawn Seraph.** *Eosphoros* is the Greek name for the morning star. A scholar of the old
tongue who works out what the name means has found a clue, and that is all it is. The name is free in the corpus.
*Phosphor* is the fallback.

**What he cannot hide, because it comes from what he refused:**

1. **He casts no shadow.** He refused Dark entirely. This is the tell a player can see in daylight.
2. **He is never seen resting**, because he cannot rest (lore).
3. **He cannot lie** (lore). He avoids questions by saying something true that is more interesting than the answer. A
   player who asks him one plain yes-or-no question and insists on the answer can corner him.
4. ⛔ **He bleeds light, not gold.** Canon already makes a Seraph gold: Caelum Vaunt's skin is *"the grey of cooled ash
   where it was gold,"* and the Seraphic Orders refine wild nanite into ordered nanite. **Proposed canon: a Seraph
   bleeds gold, because it is ordered nanite. Eosphor bleeds white light that does not pool.** Only drawn blood shows
   it, which makes it the hardest tell to get and the only one that is conclusive.

⚠️ **Precursor or fallen Seraph? The lore settles one half and leaves the other open.** The lore says the Sovereigns are
*"not the Precursors' equivalents in origin… their opposite in kind"* and *"achieved, not innate — things that
BECAME."* So a Precursor Lucifer contradicts written canon. **A fallen Seraph does not. He fits exactly.** He was the
first and brightest Seraph, and he refused the Dark so completely that it made him a Sovereign. He is ancient, and he
became what he is. And a Seraph is the one mask he can wear flawlessly, because it is what he used to be. Caelum Vaunt
is also a fallen Seraph, but a different fall: he fell into nanite logic, while Lucifer fell by refusing. The Orders'
belief that *"he is the enemy"* then reads as a family matter. (§7.2)

## §5 — ⚑ THE STARLESS WANTS THE VOID, SO IT HATES LUCIFER

The lore has already drawn this. The Void is *"what their refusing left, and they live in it."* Erik's Umbral ruling
adds: *"shadow came when light came to the Void."*

➡️ **So Lucifer is the reason the Void is not total.** Everywhere his light reaches, there is shadow, which is
relative dark, and relative dark is not the Void. **The Starless wants a dark with nothing in it to compare against, and
his light makes that impossible.** Its fear is being looked at, which is his hunger. They are opposites, and both are
unrestrained.

- **In play:** the Starless puts out lights, and in doing so it starves Lucifer better than any hero does. It is his
  most effective counterweight, and it is a villain. ⚑ **The enemy of my enemy, with no ally on either side.** A player
  who helps it in order to hurt Lucifer is feeding the Void.
- **Trajectory, per the lore's warning that "one axis with two claimants" keeps the seven from being tidy:** if Lucifer
  is slain or recrosses, the Dark half has nobody holding it back. The Starless is the one waiting to take it.
- **Staged:** `the_starless.rivals += lucifer`. Its existing rivals, sunwrack_valen and the_deep_lantern, stay.

## §6 — BROADENING THE SUPPLY LINES: A GENERATOR RULE, CAPPED

> **Erik:** *"They might need to be broadened into more areas, but I'm not sure."*

My recommendation is yes, by rule and within a cap. The rule runs whenever the generator mints a power in a new region:

- It matches the power against the three hungers:
  - **being seen:** arenas, boards, heralds, and orders that inform
  - **petition:** outlaw crowns looking for a grant, lordships with a contested claim, and guilds that take tribute
  - **bodies:** guilds that fence or smuggle, and orders that lean toward Mind
- A power that matches **may** become a line, but only if the region's stage on the arc feeding that Sovereign is at
  least 1. This keeps R40b.2 intact: the lines grow from the arc, not the arc from the lines.
- ⛔ **Cap: at most one line per Sovereign per region.** The lore says to thin these out. A world where every guild is a
  line becomes a lookup table, and the lore calls a lookup table inert.
- Every generated line carries its Sovereign's marks from §3, so what the player learns keeps paying off.
